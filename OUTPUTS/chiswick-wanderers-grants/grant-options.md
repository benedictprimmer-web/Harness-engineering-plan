# Chiswick Wanderers — grant options

Researched 18 September 2026.

**Club profile used for this research:** adult / open-age football club, registered charity or CIO, based in Chiswick (London Borough of Hounslow, Middlesex FA area), looking for under £2,000 towards facilities, kit and equipment, and coaching qualifications.

**Two caveats before you read on.** First, the funders' own websites were unreachable from this session, so the figures and dates below come from search results and intermediary sites. Check each one on the funder's site before you write anything. Second, there is no public trace of a club called "Chiswick Wanderers" — the Chiswick clubs that show up are Chiswick FC and Polytechnic FC. If the club trades under a different registered name, that name and charity number are what funders will check.

---

## Update, 18 September — confirmed club facts

Three answers from the club change the picture:

- **No junior members or junior activity.** Cash4Clubs stays in, and it is now the clear first application.
- **The club has just started.** No filing history and probably no annual accounts yet. This closes the funders that require two years of accounts and makes the small, fast routes the right order of attack. It also opens one fund that only exists for new teams: Grow the Game.
- **Pitches are rented.** No freehold, lease or licence, which removes facilities and capital funding almost entirely: Football Foundation capital grants, the Grass Pitch Maintenance Fund and Screwfix all need tenure or a building you control. Treat the £2,000 as kit, equipment and coaching only.

**Revised order:** Grow the Game and Cash4Clubs first, then Hounslow and Awards for All, then Movement for Good as a free side bet. Facilities funding is a conversation for a later season, once you have a pitch agreement and a year of accounts.

### Grow the Game — £1,500 per new team (added)

The Football Foundation's Grow the Game scheme funds the creation of new teams and coaching qualifications with grants of £1,500 per team, up to four teams, spread across three years. It is revenue funding, so renting pitches is not a barrier. Historically it has covered male teams from U14s to adult, female teams from U7s upward, and disability teams, and applicants must be not-for-profit organisations planning to set up one or more new teams.

It is the best structural fit on this list for a club in its first season, and it funds exactly what you asked about: new team costs and coaching badges.

The catch: the Football Foundation website was unreachable from this session, so the 2026-27 window, the current eligibility for open-age men's teams and the application dates all need confirming. Ask Middlesex FA — they handle these applications with clubs and will know whether the window is open.

https://footballfoundation.org.uk/grant/grow-the-game

### Aldi Sports Fund — missed this year

Up to £3,000 for grassroots clubs in England and Wales, covering equipment, kit, coaching and facility hire. Applications ran 3–31 August 2026, so you are a fortnight too late. Tiered awards from £750 to £3,000, applied for by a 200-word email to sportsfund@aldi.co.uk. Diarise late July 2027.

https://www.aldi.co.uk/sportsfund

### What being new does not block

- **Cash4Clubs** asks for a governing document or charity number and a bank statement in the club's name from the last three months. No accounts required.
- **Awards for All** does not require annual accounts if the organisation is less than 15 months old, and accepts a bank welcome letter instead of a statement if the account was opened within the last three months.

Both are designed to be reachable by a club in year one. Use them before the funders that weigh delivery history.

## The short version

Your strongest single option is **Cash4Clubs**. It is £2,000, it is unrestricted, and it is one of very few funds that *requires* you to be adults-only rather than penalising you for it. It opens on 1 October 2026, two weeks from now.

Everything else is either smaller, slower, or needs the ask reframed away from football.

## 1. Cash4Clubs — £2,000

Run by the charity Sported, funded by Flutter UKI. The 2026 pot is £500,000 across roughly 250 clubs.

**Criteria**
- Registered charity, CIC, or a club with a constitution and structured governance. You qualify.
- **Adults only.** You cannot apply if the club has junior members or runs any junior activity. This is the clause that matters most — check it against your actual membership before you spend time on the form.
- You must show sport used for social purpose: working in communities facing disadvantage, reaching adults from under-represented groups, or tackling issues like crime and anti-social behaviour.
- One grant per club. You cannot put in separate bids for different teams.

**Why it suits you**
The money is unrestricted, so a single successful bid can cover kit, a coaching course and a piece of equipment without you having to justify each line to a funder that only cares about one of them.

**The hard part**
The social purpose test. Chiswick is not an obvious deprivation story, so the case has to be about who plays rather than where you play: adults who were inactive before joining, men's mental health, players priced out of pay-to-play leagues, over-35s or walking football, whatever is actually true of your squad. Have numbers ready. Vague community benefit gets declined.

**Action:** applications open 1 October. Draft the social purpose section now, in the two weeks before it opens, because that is the part that takes thought.

https://sported.org.uk/cash4clubs/

## 2. Benefact Movement for Good — £1,000

A prize draw rather than an application. Anyone can nominate a registered charity; one nomination per charity per person, so the more members who nominate, the more entries.

Nominations run 1 January to 16 December 2026. Winners for the third draw are announced daily from 1–16 December. Once nominated you are entered into every remaining draw that year.

Costs you nothing but a WhatsApp message to the squad. Do it this week.

https://movementforgood.com/

## 3. Hounslow Thriving Communities Fund — up to £1,500

Your council fund. Small grants of up to £1,500 for local projects, plus up to £200 for one-off community events. Decisions are made at grant review meetings across the year, so applications are effectively rolling.

**Criteria**
- Charities, voluntary and community groups, informal residents' groups.
- Focus on health, wellbeing and reducing social isolation, community participation, stronger local networks.
- Priority goes to projects in the borough's Equality Opportunity Areas, which are concentrated east of Chiswick. This works against you unless you can show you draw players from those areas or run sessions there.

**How to pitch it**
Not "we need kit". Instead: a weekly adult session that gets inactive residents moving and cuts isolation, with the kit and equipment as the cost of running it. Same money, different framing.

Ealing and Hounslow CVS will help with the application for free: 020 3096 4250, iain@ehcvs.org.uk.

https://www.hounslow.gov.uk/smallgrants

## 4. National Lottery Awards for All England — £300 to £20,000

The most flexible fund on this list. Under £10,000 with a project finishing inside 12 months is the simplest route, and there is no deadline.

**Criteria**
- Registered charities are eligible. You need at least two unconnected people on the committee and a bank account requiring two signatories.
- Sport is eligible, but the project has to show benefit to the community beyond the sport itself. A club that only serves its own playing members is a weak bid.
- Smaller-income organisations are prioritised.
- Apply at least 12 weeks before you want to spend anything.

**Note:** you could ask for more than £2,000 here. If your real need is larger and £2,000 is just what you thought you could get, this is the fund where a bigger, better-argued project is no harder to win than a small one.

https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/funding-programmes/national-lottery-awards-for-all-england

## 5. Coaching qualifications — FA and Middlesex FA

The FA Grassroots Coaching Bursary gives £600 towards the FA Level 3 (UEFA B) course, targeted at female coaches and male coaches from Black, Asian and minority ethnic backgrounds. It does not guarantee a course place; you still apply for the course separately.

Below UEFA B level, course subsidies for Level 1, safeguarding and first aid tend to be handled county by county and are often not advertised. Call Middlesex FA and ask directly what is available this season for an adult club. This is the single highest-return phone call on the list, because county FA staff also know which Football Foundation windows are about to open.

https://www.middlesexfa.com/coaches/coaching-support/coaching-bursaries

## 6. Football Foundation — right funder, wrong size

The Football Foundation is the main funder of grassroots football capital work: goalposts, storage containers, portable floodlights, changing rooms, pitch drainage. Grants run up to £25,000 and typically cover up to 75% of project cost, with the club finding the remaining 25%.

**Why it does not fit a £2,000 ask**
- The programmes are built for capital items above your budget, and the 75% cap means a £2,000 grant implies a project you part-fund anyway.
- The Grass Pitch Maintenance Fund needs security of tenure — freehold, a 10-year uninterruptible lease or licence, or written landowner consent — plus a PitchPower pitch assessment report. If you rent council pitches by the session, you will not clear that bar.

**Where it does fit**
If the facilities need is real (goalposts, a storage container, changing room work) and the site tenure is there, this is the fund to build towards, not today's £2,000 bid. Route the conversation through Middlesex FA rather than applying cold.

https://footballfoundation.org.uk/looking-for-funding

## 7. Sport England Movement Fund — £300 to £15,000

Read the exclusion first: the Movement Fund will not fund football-only projects where the Football Foundation could help, which explicitly covers equipment such as goalposts and portable floodlights, and facilities.

It will fund football where the activity delivers wider social, community and health outcomes — walking football and recreational formats are the examples Sport England itself gives.

So this is available to you only if the project genuinely is a participation programme aimed at inactive or under-represented adults, and the equipment is incidental. If the honest answer is "we want kit for our league side", it is not eligible and a rejection costs you the six to eight week wait.

Apply at least 12 weeks before spending. Governance requirements tighten above £10,000.

https://www.sportengland.org/funding-and-campaigns/our-funding/movement-fund

## 8. Screwfix Foundation — up to £5,000

Capital grants to charities and not-for-profits to fix, repair, maintain and improve buildings and community facilities for people in need. Typical projects: lighting and heating efficiency, kitchens and bathrooms, painting and decorating, building safety and security.

**Only worth it if** you hold or control a building — a clubhouse or changing block. It does not fund salaries, vehicles, rent, sponsorship, electronics or easily removable equipment, so kit and balls are out.

You must be able to answer the "people in need" test (financial hardship, sickness, disability or other disadvantage). No reapplication within two years of a previous grant.

Next deadline 10 November 2026, decision by end of December. Four rounds a year.

https://www.screwfix.com/landingpage/screwfix-foundation

## 9. Heathrow Community Trust — up to £15,000

Hounslow is one of its nine boroughs. In 2026 it funded up to two years where the ask is £5,000 a year or more, across programmes for young people, environment and sustainability, and Communities Together.

Too large and too slow for a £2,000 need, and the young-people strands do not fit an adult club. Worth a look at the 2027 round guidelines if the facilities ambition grows. Round deadlines fall early in the year, so diarise a check in January.

https://www.heathrowcommunitytrust.org/apply-for-a-grant

## 10. Funds to skip, and why

- **Tesco Stronger Starts** (£500–£1,500): awards go to projects benefiting children, young people and families. An adult-only club is a weak fit even though the amount is right.
- **Asda Foundation Local Community Spaces** (£10,000–£20,000): requires annual income between £40,000 and £400,000, two years of full accounts, and a community space open all year. The 2026 round closed in April.
- **Women's and Girls' Football Fund** (£1,500–£25,000, Football Foundation): only relevant if you start a women's team, which would make it the best-funded option on this page. Flagged in case that is on the table.

## What funders will ask for

Get this pack together once and every application gets faster.

- Governing document or constitution, and your charity or CIO registration number
- Last two years of annual accounts (Asda and several trusts require this; most others ask)
- Bank account in the club's name requiring two signatories
- Safeguarding policy covering adults at risk, and your DBS position
- Written quotes for anything you are buying, usually two or three
- Landlord consent or lease terms for anything attached to a site
- Numbers: members, weekly participants, postcodes they come from, how many were inactive before joining

That last line is what separates funded bids from declined ones. Most clubs cannot produce it. If you can, say so early in every application.

## Suggested order

1. **This week.** Nominate the club for Movement for Good and get members to do the same. Call Middlesex FA about coaching course subsidies and the current Football Foundation windows. Confirm the club genuinely has no junior members.
2. **Before 1 October.** Draft the Cash4Clubs social purpose case and collect the participation numbers.
3. **1 October.** Submit Cash4Clubs on day one. It is competitive and the pot is fixed.
4. **October.** Submit the Hounslow small grant, framed around health and isolation rather than kit. Ask Ealing and Hounslow CVS to read it first.
5. **November onwards.** If the need is bigger than £2,000, build an Awards for All bid instead of stacking small ones. If you hold a building, the Screwfix deadline is 10 November.

A realistic outcome from this list is £2,000 to £4,500 over the next six months, most likely Cash4Clubs plus one of Hounslow or Movement for Good.
