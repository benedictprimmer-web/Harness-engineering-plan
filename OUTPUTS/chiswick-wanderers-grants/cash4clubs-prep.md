# Cash4Clubs — what you need

£2,000, unrestricted. Opens Thursday 1 October 2026, closes Friday 1 January 2027. Run by the charity Sported, funded by Flutter UK & Ireland. 2026 pot is £500,000 across roughly 250 clubs.

Figures and dates below come from search results, not the funder's own site, which was unreachable from this session. Confirm on sported.org.uk/cash4clubs before you submit.

---

## Eligibility — you clear all four

1. **Structure.** A club with a constitution and structured governance, a registered charity, or a company without share capital. Sole traders and companies limited by shares cannot apply.
2. **Adults only.** No junior members, no junior activity of any kind. Confirmed.
3. **Sport for social purpose.** This is the one you have to argue, not just tick.
4. **One application per club.** You cannot bid separately for different teams.

---

## Documents

Four things. That's it.

- [ ] **Governing document** — constitution or articles of association. They will also accept your charity or company number in its place.
- [ ] **Bank statement in the club's name**, dated within the last three months, clearly showing sort code and account number. The account name must match the applying organisation. They cannot pay into a personal account.
- [ ] **Safeguarding policy**, current, if you work with vulnerable adults.
- [ ] Nothing else. No annual accounts, no reserves policy, no landlord letters.

If the bank account is new or the constitution isn't formally adopted yet, fix that before 1 October. Those are the only two things that can stop you.

---

## The social purpose case

This decides the application. The examples Cash4Clubs itself gives are: operating in communities facing disadvantage, engaging adults from under-represented groups, and tackling issues such as crime and anti-social behaviour.

Chiswick is not a deprivation story, so don't try to make it one. Assessors know W4. Build the case on **who plays and what it changes for them**, which is a legitimate route to the same criterion.

Lines that work, if they're true:

- Adults who were doing no regular physical activity before they joined. Give the number.
- Men's mental health, isolation, adults who moved to the area and know nobody.
- Players priced out of pay-to-play leagues, and what you charge instead.
- Under-represented groups actually in your squad.
- Over-35s and over-50s returning to sport after years away.
- Players travelling in from the less affluent parts of Hounslow. Postcode districts make this concrete.

Lines that get declined: "we promote football in the community", "we need kit to look professional", anything about league position or ambition.

**Template to fill in**

> Chiswick Wanderers runs [x] adult teams with [y] playing members, training weekly at [venue]. Of our [y] members, [z] were doing no regular physical activity before joining, and [n] are aged over 35. Members travel in from [postcode districts]. We charge [£] per player per season against running costs of [£], deliberately below the local pay-to-play rate, because [reason]. The grant would fund [single specific thing], which would [specific measurable change].

Fill every bracket with a real number. An application with numbers beats an identical one without.

## Decide the single ask

The money is unrestricted, so you could spend it across kit, a coaching course and equipment. But the application reads better with one clear purpose and a costed quote behind it. Pick the thing that unblocks the most:

- **Coaching qualification for one or two people** — permanent capability, and it makes the next application stronger.
- **Kit and training equipment** — visible, easy to quote, weakest story on its own.
- **Pitch hire subsidy** — keeps subs low, which is the strongest social-purpose link of the three given you rent.

My read: lead with pitch hire or coaching, mention kit as a secondary line. Kit alone is the version assessors see most often.

---

## Conditions if you win

- A short feedback form is a condition of funding, not optional.
- You may be selected for a grant audit, so keep invoices and receipts for everything the grant pays for. Start that folder when the money lands, not when they ask.

---

## One thing for the trustees

The fund is money from Flutter Entertainment, a gambling company. That is not a problem for most clubs, but you are a registered charity, and trustees should record in the minutes that they considered the source and agreed to accept it. If any member or sponsor would object, better to know in October than in March. Charity Commission guidance on accepting donations is the reference point if anyone pushes back.
